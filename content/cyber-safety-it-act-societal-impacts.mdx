# Cyber Safety, IT Act 2000, Intellectual Property Rights, and Societal Impacts

In both **BPSC School Teacher Recruitment (TRE 1.0 & TRE 4.0)** and **NCERT Computer Science curricula**, societal impacts and legal frameworks are heavily evaluated. Candidates must understand **digital footprints**, **intellectual property rights (IPR)**, **open source licensing models (GPL, MIT, Creative Commons)**, and the key provisions of the **Information Technology Act, 2000 (India)**.

---

## 1. Digital Footprints, Cyber Ethics & Netiquette

### Digital Footprints
A **digital footprint** is the trail of data created while using the Internet:
- **Active Digital Footprint**: Data released deliberately by the user (e.g. social media posts, blog comments, emails, filling online registration forms).
- **Passive Digital Footprint**: Data collected without the user's explicit awareness or direct submission (e.g. browsing history, IP address logs, cookies, server tracking scripts, geolocational coordinates).

### Cyber Ethics & Online Threats
- **Phishing**: Fraudulent attempts to obtain sensitive information (passwords, credit card numbers) by disguising as a trustworthy entity in electronic communication.
  - *Spear Phishing*: Targeted phishing attack directed at specific individuals or companies.
  - *Vishing*: Phishing conducted over voice calls.
  - *Smishing*: Phishing conducted via SMS text messages.
- **Cyberstalking**: Use of electronic communication to repeatedly harass, monitor, or threaten an individual.
- **Cyberbullying**: The use of digital devices to deliberately send hateful, intimidating, or defamatory content to harm another person.
- **Ransomware**: Malicious software that encrypts user data and demands cryptocurrency payment for the decryption key.

## 2. Intellectual Property Rights (IPR)

**Intellectual Property (IP)** refers to creations of the human mind: inventions, literary and artistic works, designs, symbols, names, and images used in commerce.

```
                         Intellectual Property Rights (IPR)
                                       │
        ┌──────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┐
        ▼                              ▼                              ▼
    Copyright                       Patent                        Trademark
(Literary, Code, Music)    (Inventions & Processes)       (Logos, Brands, Slogans)
```

| Type of IPR | Protected Subject Matter | Term / Duration in India | Governing Statute |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Copyright** | Original literary, dramatic, musical, artistic works, cinematograph films, sound recordings, and **computer software source/object code**. | Lifetime of author + 60 years | Copyright Act, 1957 |
| **Patent** | Novel inventions with an inventive step capable of industrial application (new products or manufacturing processes). | 20 years from filing date | Patents Act, 1970 |
| **Trademark** | Distinctive signs, words, symbols, logos, or packaging that distinguish goods/services of one enterprise from others. | 10 years (renewable indefinitely every 10 years) | Trade Marks Act, 1999 |
| **Trade Secret** | Confidential business information, algorithms, formulas, or client lists providing competitive advantage. | Indefinite (as long as kept secret) | Common Law & Contracts |

### Plagiarism vs. Copyright Infringement
- **Plagiarism**: An ethical offense—stealing someone else's work, ideas, or words and presenting them as one's own without attribution. Not necessarily an illegal violation of law if the work is in the public domain.
- **Copyright Infringement**: A legal violation—using, reproducing, or distributing copyrighted material without authorization from the copyright holder, violating statutory legal rights.

### Fair Use Doctrine (Section 52 of Indian Copyright Act)
Permits limited use of copyrighted material without permission for purposes such as:
1. Private or personal use (including research).
2. Criticism or review.
3. Reporting of current events and news.
4. Educational instruction in certified institutions.

## 3. Open Source Software & Licensing Models

### Free Software vs. Open Source Software (FOSS)
Founded by **Richard Stallman** under the Free Software Foundation (FSF) in 1985, defined by the **Four Essential Freedoms**:
- **Freedom 0**: The freedom to run the program for any purpose.
- **Freedom 1**: The freedom to study how the program works and adapt it to your needs (access to source code).
- **Freedom 2**: The freedom to redistribute copies to help your neighbor.
- **Freedom 3**: The freedom to improve the program and release your improvements to the public.

### Software License Types
1. **Copyleft Licenses (Strong Share-Alike)**:
   - **GNU General Public License (GPL)**: Requires any derivative work or modified software to also be distributed under the exact same GPL terms with full source code disclosure.
2. **Permissive Licenses**:
   - **MIT License / BSD License / Apache 2.0**: Extremely permissive. Allows proprietary commercial redistribution and modification without requiring derivative source code to be opened.
3. **Proprietary / Closed Source**:
   - Software distributed under End User License Agreements (EULA) where source code is confidential (e.g. Microsoft Windows, Adobe Photoshop).
4. **Freeware vs Shareware**:
   - **Freeware**: Available free of monetary cost, but source code is NOT provided (e.g. Adobe Acrobat Reader).
   - **Shareware**: Proprietary software provided free on a trial basis, requiring payment for full functionality after a trial period.

### Creative Commons (CC) Licenses
Designed for creative and educational content (text, images, media):
- **CC BY (Attribution)**: Others can distribute, remix, and build upon work even commercially, provided creator is credited.
- **CC SA (ShareAlike)**: Derivative works must be licensed under identical terms.
- **CC NC (NonCommercial)**: Cannot be used for commercial profit.
- **CC ND (NoDerivatives)**: Can be redistributed, but cannot be modified or remixed.

## 4. The Information Technology Act, 2000 (India)

The **Information Technology Act, 2000 (IT Act 2000)** (amended substantially in 2008) is the primary law in India dealing with cybercrime and electronic commerce, enacted on 17 October 2000 based on the UNCITRAL Model Law on Electronic Commerce.

### Foundational Objectives
1. Legal recognition for electronic transactions and digital records.
2. Legal sanction and validation of digital signatures.
3. Facilitation of electronic filing of documents with government bodies (e-Governance).
4. Penal mechanisms to prevent and punish computer-related crimes.

### Crucial Sections and Penal Provisions (BPSC TRE Core Focus)

| Section | Offense / Subject | Maximum Penalty / Imprisonment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Section 43** | Penalty for unauthorized access, data theft, downloading, copying, or introducing virus/malware into computer systems. | Civil damages up to ₹1 crore by way of compensation to victim. |
| **Section 65** | Tampering with computer source documents / source code. | Imprisonment up to 3 years and/or fine up to ₹2 lakh. |
| **Section 66** | Computer related offenses (hacking, fraudulently causing damage or denial of service). | Imprisonment up to 3 years and/or fine up to ₹5 lakh. |
| **Section 66A** | Sending offensive messages through communication services. | **Struck down by Supreme Court of India in 2015 (Shreya Singhal v. Union of India) as unconstitutional violating Article 19(1)(a) Free Speech.** |
| **Section 66B** | Dishonestly receiving stolen computer resource or communication device. | Imprisonment up to 3 years and/or fine up to ₹1 lakh. |
| **Section 66C** | Identity theft (fraudulent use of electronic signature, password, or unique identification). | Imprisonment up to 3 years and/or fine up to ₹1 lakh. |
| **Section 66D** | Cheating by personation by using computer resource (phishing scams). | Imprisonment up to 3 years and/or fine up to ₹1 lakh. |
| **Section 66E** | Violation of bodily privacy (capturing/publishing private images without consent). | Imprisonment up to 3 years and/or fine up to ₹2 lakh. |
| **Section 66F** | **Cyber Terrorism** (threatening the unity, integrity, security or sovereignty of India, or unauthorized access to restricted defense/nuclear databases). | **Imprisonment for life**. |
| **Section 67** | Publishing or transmitting obscene material in electronic form. | 1st conviction: up to 3 years + fine up to ₹5 lakh; 2nd conviction: up to 5 years + fine up to ₹10 lakh. |
| **Section 67A** | Publishing or transmitting sexually explicit acts in electronic form. | 1st conviction: up to 5 years + fine up to ₹10 lakh; 2nd conviction: up to 7 years + fine up to ₹10 lakh. |
| **Section 70** | Unauthorized access to a designated 'Protected System' (critical national infrastructure). | Imprisonment up to 10 years and fine. |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **CERT-In (Section 70B)**: The **Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In)** operates as the national nodal agency for responding to computer security incidents, issuing security alerts, and coordinating incident response across India.

## 5. E-Waste Management & Environmental Impacts

- **E-Waste (Electronic Waste)**: Discarded electronic equipment (computers, mobile phones, printed circuit boards, CRTs).
- Contains hazardous toxic elements:
  - **Lead ($\text{Pb}$)**: Damages central nervous system and kidneys (found in cathode ray tubes and solder).
  - **Mercury ($\text{Hg}$)**: Found in flat panel displays and switches; causes neurological damage.
  - **Cadmium ($\text{Cd}$)**: Found in rechargeable batteries and SMD chip resistors; toxic to kidneys and bones.
- **The 3 R's of Environmental Sustainability**:
  $$\mathbf{\text{Reduce} \longrightarrow \text{Reuse} \longrightarrow \text{Recycle}}$$
- **E-Waste Management Rules (India)** enforce **Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)**, mandating electronics manufacturers to collect and recycle a fixed percentage of end-of-life electronic hardware.
